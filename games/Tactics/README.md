## Check the repository

From the repository root, run `bash tools/check-all.sh --full` to reproduce CI, or `bash tools/check-all.sh --fast` while iterating. The full mode builds and tests the .NET solution; fast mode runs the Tactics logic and game tests. Both modes run the phone tests and TypeScript build, Node playtest tool tests, Python asset and audio tests, and the lore validator and tests. The script prints a pass/fail line for each group and exits nonzero if any fails. Install .NET 10, Node 22, Python 3.11+ with Pillow and numpy, and npm dependencies are installed by the script.

The .NET group guards `games/`, `src/`, and the Tactics content consumed by their tests. The phone group guards `web/play/`; the Node group guards `tools/tactics-playtest/`; and the Python group guards `tools/tactics-assets/`, `tools/tactics-audio/`, and `content/tactics/base/lore/` through `tools/tactics-lore/`. The Godot TV end-to-end runner, `tools/tv-e2e.sh`, is a separate manual check.

ArcBox Tactics is a turn-based tactical battle game for the ArcBox party platform. `Tactics.Logic` is its rules engine: a deterministic, pure C# library (no clock, no `System.Random`, sorted collections only) that takes a battle state and a command and returns the new state and the events that happened, so every battle can be replayed from its seed. `Tactics.Logic.Tests` covers it, and `Tactics.Sim` balance-tests the content: it plays every encounter with sampled AI parties, AI on both sides, and writes a Markdown report (the latest is in `docs/superpowers/reports/`):

```
dotnet run -c Release --project games/Tactics/Tactics.Sim -- --levels 1,3,5,7,10 --sizes 4,5,6 --parties 6 --seeds 20 --out <report.md>
```

The design is in [docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-30-tactics-rules-engine-design.md](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-30-tactics-rules-engine-design.md). Third-party notices are in [THIRD_PARTY.md](THIRD_PARTY.md).

## Running the simulator

```
dotnet run -c Release --project games/Tactics/Tactics.Sim -- --levels 1,3,5,7,10 --sizes 4,5,6 --parties 6 --seeds 20 --difficulty Normal --out <report.md>
```

The sweep size follows the selected encounters, levels, party sizes, parties and seeds; elapsed time depends on the machine and current content. While iterating, use `--seeds 6`, or narrow it with `--encounters a,b` and `--levels`. `--help` lists every option. The rules are read at run time, so a content edit needs no rebuild.

The report also groups environmental reactions, same-side/self conduction damage, burning tile-rounds and terminal surface deaths by encounter and level. Downs are reported separately from deaths. Environment-off encounters produce zero environmental tallies.

Six seeds are noisy, so judge the final numbers at 20. The report flags:
- win rates outside 60–95%, and mean battle lengths outside 8–12 rounds, at each encounter's own level;
- role-band values more than ±15% from the band mean;
- abilities used in under 2% of the turns where they're legal.

`--companions N` (0 up to the smallest party size) plays the first N members of every party, in class order, with the `companion` profile that plays absent players, timed-out turns and AI fillers, instead of their class's profile. Run the same sweep with and without it to compare (#109). A companion's weights are those of its class's profile (`healer`, `caster`, `archer`, `skirmisher` or `brute`, chosen from the class's `aiTags`), with the companion-only rules on top: it revives first, spends class resources freely, guards healers and protected allies, and uses at most one healing item.

Simulated parties are human and have no subclass. To measure what a subclass or race adds, `--subclasses rogue:rogue.shadow,monk:monk.windstep` gives each listed class that subclass, and `--race elf` makes every party member that race (#110).

To tune one class faster, `--with-class rogue` plays only the sampled parties that include it (the same parties, so its numbers compare with a full sweep's), and `--weapons rogue:shortbow` swaps a class's starting weapon. `--policy hidden-rogue` has the party's rogues play a scripted human who sets up Sneak Attack every turn instead of the AI: it hides whenever the rules allow and strikes from hiding, using legal commands only (#108); if the engine refuses one of its commands anyway, the AI plays the rest of that turn. The report's "Sneak Attack set-up (rogues)" table gives the rogues' Hide, Sneak Attack, from-hiding and flanking rates by level, and the scripted rogue's refused commands. The latest numbers and every tuning decision are in [the final balance report](../../docs/superpowers/reports/2026-10-30-tactics-balance.md).

If a content change alters a golden replay, re-record with `UPDATE_GOLDENS=1 dotnet test games/Tactics/Tactics.Logic.Tests`, review each diff, then run again without the flag. The recording run fails `Warlord_bridge_plays_the_showcase_triggers` if it runs before the replay is written; the second run must pass. To re-record `warlord-bridge` from the AI, empty its `commands` in `Golden/warlord-bridge.replay.json`.

## Campaign difficulty (#114)

A campaign has a difficulty, Story, Normal (the default, and what a document from before it reads as) or Hard, saved in the campaign document and passed through `Encounters.Setup` into every battle's `BattleState.Difficulty`: story fights, roaming mobs and Hollow fights alike. Only the VIP can change it, from the "•••" session menu on their phone, any time outside a battle (an input during a battle is refused; a battle keeps the difficulty it began at). After a lost battle the VIP's results screen says "Too hard? After Carry on, change difficulty in the party menu", unless it is Story already. The TV battle HUD carries a small "Story" or "Hard" tag when it isn't Normal. There is no portal control: the portal has no campaign-settings page.

For testing, the Development setting `Tactics:Difficulty` (`Story`, `Normal` or `Hard`) is an override: when it is set it wins over the campaign's value for every battle (and the phone menu shows it), and the menu's changes are still saved but have no effect while it is set. It is read in Development only; leave it unset to play the campaign's own setting.

Environmental reactions are opt-in per encounter. River Crossing, Ford Assault, Forest Ruins, Warlord Bridge and Crystal Cave enable them. The [completion report](../../docs/superpowers/reports/2026-11-02-environmental-tiles/completion.md) records final Ford/cave tuning, on/off comparisons, party-size results, visual review and Chapter 1 validation. ## Bot phones and the TV, end to end

The fighting bots read the phone's optional damage forecasts when choosing a route, and leave burning ground on a safe path even after spending their action. Safe exits stay directed toward the shared target, and explicit defeat objectives trigger pursuit immediately. The bots retain the dry-ground preference among equally safe paths. This uses public phone data, not hidden battle state. Run its regression checks with `node --test tools/tactics-playtest/hazards.test.cjs`; the completion report records the older hazard-unaware attempts separately.

`tools/tactics-playtest/bots.cjs` joins a room as N phones over the real hub, with no browser, and plays a session: the first bot is the VIP, makes a campaign and starts the game, and each bot picks a class at Muster. Its header has every option and how to run the server and TV by hand; it needs `npm ci` in `web/play` first.

Muster lands on the walk screen. By default the VIP leaves it with `battles`, picks `--encounter` (default `river_crossing`) and the run is a battle to its Results:

| Option | What the bots do |
|---|---|
| `--fight` | play to win from what the phone shows (below); without it they only end their turns |
| `--lose[=<n>]` | lose the first n fights (default every one): walk up to the foes and do nothing there |
| `--classes=a,b,…` | the class each bot picks, in order (default fighter, cleric, rogue, wizard, paladin, ranger) |
| `--area`, `--sit-out=turn,react`, `--leave` | prefer area spells; let the turn and reaction timers run out; leave the battle to the AI |
| `--then=next` | play the next battle after the Results, for ever, instead of ending the session |
| `--people=<n>` | wait for n real phones to take a seat first, to play alongside the bots |
| `--host` | open the room and watch the host view themselves, with no TV |
| `--host --shown` | and answer every beat with `shown` at once, so a battle with no TV takes seconds: for trying the bots' play over many battles |
| `--walk-to-stamp[=<n>]` | fight n times (default 1) where the party walks into a fight, not from the list (below) |

**How `--fight` plays.** Each bot reads its turn screen's previews, as a player would. A friend who is down or below half health is healed first, and a healer keeps its spells for that, attacking with what costs nothing. Otherwise it takes the use with the most expected damage, a kill worth more, so the party finishes the hurt foe before spreading its blows, and never one that would hurt a friend. The party holds the ground it started on and takes on whatever comes within 7 tiles of it. Once the foes have kept away for two of the party's turns each, or two or fewer are left, it goes after them, the weakest first. It walks by the map's own ground: round the river to the ford, and never standing in water when dry ground will do. With no TV (`--host --shown`), 4 bots at level 1 won River Crossing 40 times out of 40, and the Ford Assault 4 times out of 20. The old play, which walked straight at the nearest foe and used the first ability in reach, lost River Crossing every time on the TV.

**`--walk-to-stamp`** needs to know where the party stands: the TV's `--walk-log` lines through `--tv-log`, or `--host`. From there `route.cs` (`dotnet run tools/tactics-playtest/route.cs -- <rules> <region> <map> <x> <y>`) gives the way into the stamp of `--encounter`'s map, by the server's own walking rule. The leader taps it out, a step at a time, waiting for each, and the step into the stamp starts the fight. A beat's lead-in scene plays before its fight, and its aftermath after a win: the leader's bot skips each line after 1.5 s, and the bots answer votes (the first option) and checks (the best bonus) at once. After each results the VIP goes on, so the next walk starts where the last fight left the party: after a loss, at the recovery spawn, about 100 steps away. After the last fight the party walks on a few steps, and the VIP ends the session.

Every bot logs each move, ability and end of turn, and a timeout says which screen each bot was on. With no TV nothing sends `shown`, so each AI beat waits its fallback (400 ms plus 250 ms an event, at most 6 s), and a battle takes minutes: a `--host` run with `--timeout=40` times out mid-battle with the bots still playing, which is the "no turn logged" the handoff saw.

**`--chapter`** plays all of Chapter 1 from wherever the party is, from a fresh campaign with no seed: it walks to the beat on offer, which it reads from the TV's `--story-log` (`STORY marker <beat> <x> <y> <emote>`) or the host view, by `route.cs … beat <id> <x> <y>` (round every stamp, so a battle beat's fight starts from its "!"), checking each step lands where the route said and routing again if not, and taps Interact once it's offered. In the scenes the leader skips each line after `--line` seconds (default 1.5), everyone votes for `--branch=force|parley` at the ford (and for `--story-pick`'s ids elsewhere, else the first option), and the best bonus rolls. A battle beat is fought with `--fight`; a loss puts its "!" back and the bots walk to it again. Once the TV shows `STORY card` the party walks a few steps and the VIP ends the session. `--silent` answers nothing and skips no line, so everything runs on the game's timers. With no TV (`--host --shown`) parley and force each played through in about 5 minutes. **A lost fight** (the user's rule, 2026-11-01) pays no XP, and the party wakes with that fight's injuries tended, its earlier ones as they were, and the potions it drank gone. When a loss kept its injuries, the bots lost nearly every Ford Assault retry they were seen to play after a real loss (14 of 15); with `--lose=1` (a loss that spends little) they won 6 of 12 retries under the old rule and 8 of 12 with the injuries healed. A force run is still about a coin toss on its first fight (5 of 10 won from the "!" side with no TV).

Add `--camp` to a Chapter 1 run to visit the ford campfire after the battle, vote Rest, pick Tend where a body injury needs care
(or Cook otherwise), resolve the night, and sponsor an inn room after the next story rest is earned. The script checks both recovery
visits before accepting the card. The equivalent TV script is `ARCBOX_CHAPTER=1 ARCBOX_CAMP=1 ARCBOX_BRANCH=force ARCBOX_PHONES=6 tools/tv-e2e.sh --skip-build`.

Rest mends injuries rather than replenishing HP or slots, which remain fresh every battle. Camp jobs use each player's own character;
paid inn rooms and temple cures spend the buyer's own purse and can help any campaign member. See the
[rest design](../../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-03-tactics-rest-and-recovery-design.md) for the balance defaults, saves and timers.


With any walking option the party walks instead, and then the VIP ends the session. They run in this order:

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| `--walk="e:4,ne:2,stop:1"` | holds each screen direction for so many seconds, pinging every 250 ms as the pad does; `stop` rests; `pause:<name>` logs `PAUSE <name>` and waits for the file `<name>` in `--go=<dir>` |
| `--walk-latency[=<n>]` | n presses (default 20), each timed from the press to the TV's `WALK` line in `--tv-log` (or the host view with `--host`) |
| `--walk-flood[=<n>]` | n seconds of a press and a stop at 60 messages a second, three times what the server's limiter lets through |
| `--walk-drop[=kill\|stall]` | holds, then cuts the leader's connection with no stop, and times the party stopping, the lead passing and the disconnect event |

While walking, whoever leads sends the pad's idle ping (a stop every 3 s), so a quiet leader keeps the lead as on a real phone.

The TV's dev flag `--walk-log` prints what the bots and the e2e run read:

- `WALK <seq> <x> <y> <unix ms>`: each step the TV is told of, when it reached the TV's main thread. Steps that came in the TV's first view after a snap aren't printed.
- `WALKAT <unix ms> <seq> <held 0|1> <leader x> <leader y> <widest gap> <name> <x> <y> …`: where the TV drew each walker, leader first, at most every 0.25 s while anything moves and once when it comes to rest. Places are in tiles, with a tile's middle at .50. The widest gap is the furthest any walker is from the one ahead: a follower falling behind shows here.
- `BUMP <unix ms> <leader x> <leader y>`: the leader bumped a wall, because `Bumps` went up in a view. A first view, a new epoch and a reconnecting TV don't bump. With `--shots` the first few bumps are saved as `tactics-world-bump-<x>-<y>`.
- `TACTICS walk snapped to <x> <y>`: the party was put somewhere rather than walked there: a first view, a new epoch, a reconnect.
- `WALKSTATS …`: frame times and how far things moved on screen each frame, every 288 frames of walking and once more when the party stops, so a short walk has one line and a long one several.

## Adding a chapter pack

A pack is a folder with a `manifest.json`, and each weekly chapter is its own pack (`chapter-03`, and so on). `base` is always loaded.

1. Create the folder and its `manifest.json`: `id`, `rulesVersion`, `requires` (the pack ids it needs) and `files`, the list of its content files.
2. Add content in the same formats as `content/tactics/base/rules/`, with the schemas in `schema/`. Bring maps, monsters and encounters for the chapter.
3. **A chapter may add ids, but never redefine one by accident.** An id defined twice without `"replaces": "<id>"` is a load error that names both files and the JSON pointer, so balance changes stay deliberate.
4. Set an optional intended `level` on each encounter, so the simulator judges it at that level, and aim for no more than 10 enemies: that's the AI's tested worst case.
5. Run the simulator over the new encounters at their levels.

A pack with errors doesn't load: errors carry the file and a JSON pointer. The content hash covers every loaded pack and is stored in each saved battle, so changing a pack mid-campaign is detected.

## Content conventions

- **Persistent Delay and Downed countdown (#115):** Delay keeps the unit behind the same anchor (or at the end) in later rounds; waiting cannot buy consecutive turns across a round boundary. A Downed hero loses one countdown at each own-turn start, plus hits, rather than at round start.
- **Area weapon riders (#107):** Sweeping Cleave's extra 1d8 (1d10 at level 10) applies once per use across all struck targets, through the first hit; subsequent hits use the weapon's base damage.

These turn SRD 5.2 into this game, and every content file starts from them. **The balance pass moved some numbers away from them on purpose**; the [balance report](../../docs/superpowers/reports/2026-10-30-tactics-balance.md) lists each change and its reason, and the differences are noted below.

- **Spell tier** = SRD spell level. **Mana cost = tier**; cantrips cost 0.
- **Max tier by level:** full casters (wizard, cleric, bard, druid) can cast tier ⌈level / 2⌉, capped at 5. Half casters (paladin, ranger) can cast tier ⌈level / 4⌉.
- **Pools:**
  - Full-caster Mana by level: 4, 6, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16. Bard and druid get 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 10, 11, 12 because they have a second resource.
  - Every other resource interpolates linearly between the spec §4.5 endpoints (level 1 → level 10), rounding down.
  - *After balancing:* the wizard starts with 5 Mana, then follows this table from level 2. The bard, cleric, druid and sorcerer pools and the ranger's Focus differ, and warlock spells can cost more than 1 pact.
- **Affordable on arrival:** every ability a class or subclass grants, offers in a `choose` list or puts in its `defaultPicks` can be paid for from the class's pools at the level it arrives, and pools never shrink as the level rises. Tune a cost and a pool together, or move the pick to the level its cost fits.
- **The area ladder:** within one class's Mana spells, a higher-tier area spell covering at least as many tiles never deals less direct damage per target than a lower-tier one (equal is fine: the bigger footprint is the upgrade). Two area spells of the same tier whose footprints are within a third of each other differ by at most 1.5× per target. Single-target spells, damage dealt only by a zone, secondary environmental conduction and subclass kits aren't on this ladder. Conduction depends on the terrain and nearby units, so it is measured in encounter simulations rather than counted as a spell's direct area damage. Balance tuning has to cut the whole ladder, not only the spells in `defaultPicks`.
  - `ContentLadderTests` checks both rules at every level 1–10, so they hold beyond the levels and picks the simulator samples.
- **Final SRD pass (#112):** Fireball and Lightning Bolt deal 6d6 per target (SRD Fireball is 8d6); Cone of Cold deals 8d8. This preserves a ≥1.5× per-target step from Burning Hands to Fireball to Cone of Cold. Ice Storm uses SRD 2d8 bludgeoning + 4d6 cold, Wall of Fire's initial hit is 5d8, and Cloudkill's initial hit is 6d8 (its ongoing damage remains 5d8), keeping the complete area ladder consistent. Wall of Fire's ongoing 1d8 remains a balance departure. The tooltips show all dice.
- **Retained balance decisions (#112):** monster save proficiencies, the rogue’s rapier and Hide at level 1, the bounded rogue/monk attack schedules, and the warlord bridge redesign (one collapse, survival through round 11, 240-HP goblin boss) remain. Quickened Spell arrives at level 7, where its cost is affordable, instead of the earlier SRD progression.
- **Monotonic level-up dice (#112):** monk unarmed attacks use 1d8 at every level, with two attacks per Attack action; Eldritch Blast uses 1d12 per beam throughout; bard Healing Word and Cure Wounds use 2d4 and 2d8 throughout. These game progressions and existing extra-attack/beam schedules depart from SRD; no level-up reduces direct dice. Any further balance changes must preserve that invariant.
- **Class balance tuning (#112):** the monk keeps its original Ki progression, gains Unarmored Movement at level 1 and uses two main attacks throughout; its flat d8 is a departure from SRD's growing martial-arts die, retained to bound its many bonus attacks. Heightened Flurry remains three strikes for 1 Ki; Stunning Strike costs 2 Ki for its first-hit rider. Second Wind has one use each battle. Hunter's Mark adds 1d4 at levels 1–2, then 1d6; Conjure Barrage deals 4d8 per target. Sorcerer Mana is 4/6/6/6/6/6/8/8/8/8; cleric Devotion starts at 5. Wizard defaults learn Hypnotic Pattern at level 5 and Counterspell at 6, with explicit controller priorities that rise when tier-5 damage arrives. Paladin defaults begin with Compelled Duel, and its guardian profile prioritizes closing and striking; warlock defaults use ranged striker priorities. Players retain the other spell choices.
- **Conditions and Shove (#112):** Shove offers two Actions: push 1 tile or knock prone, each against the target's better total STR/DEX save bonus (including proficiency), DC 8 + proficiency + the attacker's STR modifier. At the start of a prone unit's turn it automatically stands, spending half its available Move rounded up; with no movement or when restrained it stays prone. Automatic standing and whole-tile rounding are game approximations. Charmed prevents harmful targeting of the charmer, including area targeting, and gives no disadvantage against others. Bleeding ends only when healing actually restores HP (`onHealed`, after revival); zero healing and healing at full HP do not end it. Put out flames spends the Action to end Burning on self or an adjacent ally; water still extinguishes it through the environment rules. Uncanny Dodge halves only the triggering numbered attack hit, including riders and all damage blocks, rounded down once after resistance; it grants no temporary HP. The “only when it changes the outcome” preference uses an isolated deterministic replay of that hit and takes Dodge only if it prevents Downing.
- **Striker measurement (#112):** bands use damage per target-turn: sum each party member's own-turn mean HP damage per damaged hostile, then divide by battle rounds. Hits on one victim accumulate before averaging, and off-turn damage is assigned to the source's latest own turn. Raw damage per round remains in simulator reports for audit. Damage before the first own turn joins that turn, and resuming a delayed turn keeps its original bucket. The final audit enables `--rotate-formation`, which cycles spawn seats across seeds without consuming RNG; the legacy simulator default retains fixed class order. This prevents extra area victims from multiplying band credit; splitting attacks across victims also receives less credit.
- **Spell ranges:** touch → 1. SRD range ≤ 30 ft → 4. Any longer ranged spell → 6.
- **Weapon ranges:** thrown → 4. Shortbow and light crossbow → 6. Longbow and heavy crossbow → 8.
- **Areas:** radius r ft → `burst` ⌈r / 10⌉. Cone r ft → `cone` ⌈r / 10⌉ + 1. Line r ft → `line` ⌈r / 10⌉.
- **Durations:** 1 minute → 10 rounds. 1 hour or longer → the whole battle. "Save ends" stays `until save`.
  - *After balancing:* Moonbeam lasts 1 round.
- **Per-rest features:** short-rest and long-rest uses both become `usesPerBattle`. Pools such as Lay on Hands refill each battle.
- **Left out:** spells with no battle use (utility, ritual, travel, divination).
- **`defaultPicks`:** every class lists a preset loadout per level, used by the simulator and by AI-built characters. Real players choose their own in the portal (sub-project 4).
- **Content may add, never override:** an id defined twice without `"replaces"` is a load error.
- **Retreat (#111):** from round 3 on, any present player can propose "Fall back" from their phone, on anyone's turn. A strict majority of the present players ends the battle as `BattleOutcome.Fled` (the `Retreat` command): half XP, injuries kept, no loot, and the story counts it as not won (the beat waits, as after a loss). The vote lives in the adapter and times out after 20 s; a failed vote can't be proposed again until the next round. There is no exit-zone requirement. An encounter opts out with `"retreat": false` (default true); none of the shipped ones does.
- **Multi-attacks pick their targets (#107):** a weapon attack (`isWeaponAttack`) makes the sheet's `attacks` attacks, each with its own target. A `Use` may list one target per attack (`targets`, the phone's "Attack 2 of N"; a shorter list repeats its last entry); without a list every attack goes at the command's target. When an attack's target is Dead, Out, or can no longer be attacked (pushed out of reach), it goes to the next listed target still in reach (a listed Downed unit counts: it was chosen), then to the standing hostile in reach with the least current HP (ties by unit id); Downed units are skipped there, and with only Downed hostiles left in reach the attack is spent. An area weapon attack (Cleave) strikes each unit in its area instead. Attacks made as a reaction stay one attack.
- **Once-only riders (`riderOnce`, a departure from SRD 5.2, #107):** an ability with `"riderOnce": true` runs its whole effect list on its attacks up to and including the first that hits; the attacks after it are plain hits (its first attack roll without the bonus, dealing its first damage block, weapon damage without the extra dice). SRD manoeuvres, smites and Stunning Strike are once per attack or per turn and choose which hit; here the first hit always takes it. Every resource-costed (`resource` or `uses`) `isWeaponAttack` ability that a unit with more than one attack can use must set it: `RiderOnceTests` checks the classes (with subclasses and `choose` lists), races and monsters (with phases). The tooltip says so.
- **Stale-plan recovery (#241):** if an AI command is refused after a reaction changes resources or targets, the adapter discards the stale plan and replans once from the current remaining economy. A second refused plan ends that turn; engine exceptions retain the separate watchdog. The simulator keeps its actual refusal counts and bounded recovery, now reporting the ability and rejection code for every refusal.
- **Emergency healing and guardian control (#240):** healing a standing ally is worth the HP restored plus the expected turn preserved by avoiding Downed, using the current threat map with a conservative 0.8 confidence factor for mean healing. Full-health allies gain no such credit; a simultaneous ward covers only the remaining risk. Guardians value control at 0.4 while retaining their aggressive damage/positioning weights, so Command can beat Smite against a foe whose turn is much more dangerous. Command already denied a whole turn; the old guardian weight discounted it to near zero.
- **What the AI plans (#110):** an `empty_tile` ability is planned when it moves the user (`teleport` on itself, or `jumpTo`: aimed at the best landing tiles in reach, worth what landing there gains) or places a `summon` (aimed beside a foe); any other empty-tile effect is left alone. A teleport on its own only escapes (to a tile with less threat on it); to close in it comes before an action ("teleport, then strike"), and it never lands where the foes' expected damage next round would Down the unit. A self ability with `extraAction` `action` (Action Surge) is worth the best second action it buys, and one with `extraAction` `move` is a dash, planned with the action or the bonus action. The `archer` and `skirmisher` profiles also walk out, act and walk back (move–act–move). Twinned and Careful Spell are planned before the spell they improve (a second target in range; allies the area would catch pass its save). A condition whose passive revives its holder when it is Downed (Death Ward) is worth the chance the threat map gives of the holder dropping next round, and nothing once a `usesPerBattle` limit on that passive is spent; a ward that can be cast again after it fires should end itself instead (`remove` its own condition, as `death_ward` does), not limit its uses. A later action in the turn (a surged second action, the bonus action) keeps off a target an `onAttackHit` passive of the user's may push or pull to a tile it couldn't reach the target on (out of range or sight, where the forced move would leave it), the passive's `when` checked as the engine checks it. A plan that spends the bonus action early (a disengage, a teleport, a dash) is ranked without the bonus action it would otherwise end with.
- **Fair play with Downed heroes (a departure from spec §5.4, #110):** the spec gives hitting a Downed character zero value unless the profile is `cruel`. An area (or a zone, or a conduction it sets off) that catches one would then tick that hero's countdown for free, so it now counts against the aim: what a cruel profile gains for the hit (its chance × the hero's worth ÷ the countdown left), any other profile loses. An equal aim that spares the hero wins; a much better one still goes ahead.
- **Boss guard (a departure from SRD 5.2, #106):** pushed or pulled into a deep tile (lava, chasm, river), a non-party unit dies. A *guarded* unit, one whose monster sets `"steadfast": true` or that has an `hpBelow` phase trigger in the encounter, stops on the last tile of the push that isn't deep instead (`PushResisted`, "BRACED" on the TV), or doesn't move. Set `steadfast` on every boss stat block. Damage that would take a unit past an untriggered phase threshold also leaves it at the threshold: the phase fires and the rest of that hit is lost. Heroes and ordinary enemies are unchanged. The AI scores a push by where it lands (the target's remaining HP for a deep tile; nothing for a guarded unit) and avoids ending a turn on an edge tile next to deep with a foe able to shove it over.
- **Hiding (a departure from SRD 5.2, #108):** the rogue's Hide (`rogue.cunning_hide`, a bonus action from level 1) needs no Stealth roll. It is refused in *plain view*: when a foe that can act stands next to the rogue, or sees it (line of sight from the foe's tile) with no cover. Walls, props, height and sight-blocking zones such as smoke give cover; standing units don't, and half cover is enough (SRD 5.2 asks for heavy obscurement or three-quarters cover and a Stealth check). While *Hiding* (the `hiding` condition), attacks against the rogue have disadvantage and its next attack has advantage; it ends when the rogue attacks, when it ends its turn in plain view, or after its next turn. Steady Aim is folded into it: there is no separate Steady Aim. The ranger's Vanish, the warlock's One with Shadows and Invisibility still apply the older `hidden`, which only an attack ends. Content can put such a test on any ability with `"requires"` (a `when`; the `seen` leaf is plain view): the engine refuses the ability otherwise (`RejectCode.Unmet`, "Only when you aren't in plain view.") and the tooltip says so. It is a test on the user alone, checked before anything is aimed or rolled, so the loader refuses a leaf that reads an attack, a roll or a target (`weapon`, `advantage`, `disadvantage`, `success`, `flanked`, `allyBesideTarget`, `heightAbove`, `isSpell`, `damageType`).
- **Flanking (a departure from SRD 5.2, which has no flanking rule):** a melee attack on a target with one of the attacker's allies that can act on the tile directly opposite it has advantage, for every class and monster, as it has since the rules engine was built. The hobgoblin's Martial Advantage counts it too, and Sneak Attack counts the advantage it gives. #108 kept it for everyone: it is already inside every role band.
- **Rogue weapon budgets (#239):** an Attack action makes two melee attacks or one ranged attack at every level. Melee has the extra attack to compensate for closing distance; ranged retains its reach, Vex and the ally clause for Sneak Attack. Sneak Attack progresses through 2d6 / 2d6+2 / 5d6 / 7d6 at levels 1 / 3 / 5 / 9, within the existing once-per-turn caps and without a level-up damage loss. The level-3/4 step is a documented balance departure. A class may optionally author a separate `rangedAttacks` feature progression; other classes inherit their existing attack count. The phone guide and equipped weapon show the actual count.
- **The rogue (#108):** at most two attacks per Attack action, from level 1. Its once-per-turn damage riders are capped by the #108 ruling at 2d6 at levels 1–2, 3d6 at 3–4, 5d6 at 5–8 and 7d6 at 9–10, Sneak Attack and any subclass passive like it together, and Sneak Attack deals exactly the cap. `RogueContentTests` holds both bounds, summing the riders for the rogue and every subclass at every level. Shadow's Death from the Shadows (level 10) used to add 2d6 necrotic on a hit with advantage, 9d6 with Sneak Attack; it now frightens the target for a round instead (Wisdom save against the rogue's Dexterity DC), because the engine has no shared once-per-turn budget or per-subclass Sneak Attack to fold extra dice into. Sneak Attack follows SRD 5.2: advantage, or an ally that can act next to the target while the rogue doesn't have disadvantage (it used to need a flank). The rogue has SRD 5.2's Vex weapon mastery (`rogue.vex`): a hit with a dart, rapier, shortbow or shortsword makes the target *Vexed* (the `vexed` condition) until the end of the rogue's next turn, and the rogue's attacks on a target it vexed have advantage. Vex is the rogue's own mark: it never takes the place of another class's `marked` (a paladin's Vow of Enmity keeps its target and advantage), and the passives that read `marked`, such as Vengeful Strikes and Ferocious Bond, ignore it. Departures: the mark helps every attack until then, not just the next one, and no other mastery or class's Weapon Mastery is modelled. Cunning Action: Disengage comes at level 3, where Steady Aim was. These numbers fit the AI's play, which rarely hides; the simulator's scripted always-hidden rogue (`--policy hidden-rogue`) is the bound for a human who sets up Sneak Attack every turn ([rogue report](../../docs/superpowers/reports/2026-11-06-combat-98/rogue.md)).

### Optional roaming encounters (#79)

Seven manifest-listed groups inhabit Ashen Ford and Cinder Hollow. They wander connected patches without chasing; leader contact triggers the matching painted arena. Followers do not trigger fights. Wins pay 75 XP per member and split 10 gold among present players, with no loot or same-chapter respawn. Full sweep: 525 XP per member and 70 gold total. Groups reset only on a later chapter. Story-only routes remain avoidable; validation reserves the entire roaming area and contact distance when proving safe routes.
